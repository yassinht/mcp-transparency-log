Verifiable by anyone, including your auditor

Every night's observations are leaves of an RFC 6962 Merkle tree with a signed head. The leaf hashes are published. A clean clone and one command rebuild the tree and check every head ever signed.

  • Same construction as the Go checksum database
  • Hash specified with test vectors; a second observer reproduces it
  • Cross-checked nightly against independent crawlers

FAQ

Questions a careful buyer asks.

Do you call the tools on servers you observe?

No. The crawler sends initialize and tools/list and nothing else. It never executes a tool, never installs a package, never sends credentials to anyone. Behavioural testing exists only as a consented service on servers you own.

Can it watch our private or authenticated servers?

Yes, from your CI: a header per server from an environment variable, sent on every request and written nowhere. The public log itself covers only unauthenticated servers; your private checks leave no trace here. A private log that runs inside your network is being built.

What does verification actually prove?

That the published heads were signed by the key holder, that each is consistent with the published leaves, and that no observation was altered or removed after a head committing to it was published. It does not by itself prove the leaves describe reality; that is what cross-checks with independent observers are for, and we publish ours nightly.

Is there a model judging anything?

No. Every judgement in the data path is a rule a human can re-run against the archived bytes. The rule scan's thirteen patterns are regular expressions in the open-source repository; every match is quoted and none is called malicious.

Why is the log called something else?

Surfacelog is the company and the product. The public record it operates is the MCP Transparency Log, which has kept that name since its first census on 30 August 2026 and keeps it, because a record's name should not change.

About

Eighty nights of paying attention.

The log started on 30 August 2026 as one question: is the MCP ecosystem observable at all, or is "trust the registry" the only option? It was. Eighty nights later it holds over two million signed observations, and the official registry's maintainers used its measurements as evidence when they designed periodic re-validation of entries.

Surfacelog is built and run by Yassine Houta, a software engineer in Tunisia, and delivered to companies with experteam.tn. The code is Apache-2.0. The design rules are simple and public: raw bytes are the record, every judgement is a rule a human can re-run, no model anywhere in the data path, and every correction ever made to the numbers is still in the README.

Other people watch the same servers. We publish our nightly observations in the format they read, because two independent observers that agree are worth more than one that asks to be believed.
